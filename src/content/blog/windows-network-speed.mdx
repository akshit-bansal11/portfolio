## In one minute

- Your speed is capped by the **narrowest** of three things: your ISP plan, your router, and the link from the router to your PC. No Windows setting can push you past your plan.
- **Measure first.** Run a speed test on Wi-Fi and on a cable. That tells you which part is slow.
- The real fix is physical: **use a cable**, or connect to **5 GHz** and sit closer to the router. Adapter settings (Prefer 5GHz, Leisure Power Save off, Roaming Aggressiveness low, Ethernet power saving off) keep the link steady and cut ping spikes. Leave the rest on default.
- Make big downloads use all of your speed with **parallel downloading** (a Chrome flag, or Ghost Downloader). Skip forced Speed & Duplex, the "20% reserved bandwidth" myth, turning off TCP auto-tuning, and booster apps.

## Where your speed actually goes

Your data passes through several hops on its way to you. Each hop has its own limit. The slowest one wins.

- **On a cable, splitting the download**, your plan is the bottleneck. That is the best you can do. Only a faster plan goes faster.
- **On 5 GHz, rooms away**, the Wi-Fi link is the bottleneck. Sitting closer helps more than any setting.
- **One connection** is often held back by the server, however fast your link is.

<Callout kind="important">

No setting in Windows makes your connection wider than your plan. Settings can only stop your PC from **wasting** what you have.

</Callout>

## Make downloads use your full speed

A lot of servers limit how fast a single connection can go. So even on a fast plan, one download can crawl. The fix: split the file into chunks and download them over several connections at once.

### Chrome: turn on parallel downloading

Chrome has a hidden setting (a flag) for this.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the flag" tone="violet">

Open a new tab and go to `chrome://flags/#enable-parallel-downloading`.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Enable it" tone="violet">

Set **Parallel downloading** to **Enabled**.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Relaunch" tone="violet" checkpoint="Chrome restarts. Large downloads are now split into several streams and joined when they finish.">

Click **Relaunch**.

  </Step>
</Steps>

- It only kicks in when the server supports split downloads. You'll notice it mostly on big files.
- Microsoft Edge has the same flag at `edge://flags/#enable-parallel-downloading`.

<Callout kind="warn">

Flags are experimental, so the option can move or disappear in future versions. If you can't find it, it may already be on by default in your version.

</Callout>

For big downloads, a dedicated download manager does the same thing, but better. [Ghost Downloader](https://github.com/XiaoYouChR/Ghost-Downloader-3) is free and open source, on GitHub.

## Changed something and it got worse? Undo it

None of these adapter changes are permanent. To reset everything at once:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Device Manager" tone="green">

Expand **Network adapters**.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Uninstall the adapter you changed" tone="green">

Right-click it and choose **Uninstall device**. Leave **Attempt to remove the driver for this device** unchecked.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Restart your PC" tone="green" checkpoint="Windows reinstalls the adapter on boot, with every setting back to its default.">

Restart as normal.

  </Step>
</Steps>

- **One setting only?** Open the **Advanced** tab and pick the original value again.
- **The Chrome flag?** Go back to `chrome://flags` and click **Reset all**.

Swap in a Cat 5e or Cat 6 cable. Check that Speed & Duplex is on **Auto Negotiation**.

  </Trouble>
  <Trouble
    symptom="Wi-Fi drops after the PC has been idle"
    cause={<p>Windows put the adapter to sleep to save power.</p>}
  >

In the adapter's **Power Management** tab, uncheck **Allow the computer to turn off this device to save power**.

  </Trouble>
  <Trouble
    symptom="Random lag spikes on Wi-Fi in games and calls"
    cause={<p>The adapter's power saving (Leisure Power Save, or MIMO Power Save Mode on Intel).</p>}
  >

Set it to **Disabled** in the **Advanced** tab.

  </Trouble>
  <Trouble
    symptom="On mesh Wi-Fi, the laptop stays stuck on a weak access point"
    cause={<p>Roaming Aggressiveness is set to Disable or Lowest, so the adapter doesn't look for a stronger one.</p>}
  >

Set **Roaming Aggressiveness** to **Medium**.

  </Trouble>
  <Trouble
    symptom="The laptop wakes up from sleep on its own"
    cause={<p>Wake on Magic Packet or Wake on Pattern Match is on.</p>}
  >

Set both to **Disabled**, unless you use Wake-on-LAN.

  </Trouble>
  <Trouble
    symptom="chrome://flags has no Parallel downloading option"
    cause={<p>Flags are experimental. It may have moved, or already be on by default in your version.</p>}
  >

Use a download manager such as Ghost Downloader instead.

  </Trouble>
  <Trouble
    symptom="The connection got worse after changing adapter settings"
    cause={<p>One of the changes doesn't suit your adapter or router.</p>}
  >

Reset with the uninstall steps above, then change only the settings in this guide.

  </Trouble>
</TroubleGroup>
